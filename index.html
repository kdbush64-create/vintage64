THE REAL PROBLEM

Your machine isn't slow. Windows is.

There's a difference between hardware that's worn out and software that's gotten too heavy to carry. Most people are dealing with the second one.

WHAT WINDOWS DOES OVER TIME

The bloat compounds.

Every Windows update adds telemetry services, background processes, and startup programs you didn't ask for. The registry grows. Scheduled tasks multiply. A machine that booted in 45 seconds when new can take four minutes after a few years of updates — not because the hardware got slower, but because the software load got heavier. Microsoft's answer is to sell you a new machine that meets their latest requirements.

WHAT LINUX ACTUALLY DOES

Fast, lean, and yours to keep.

Linux doesn't run background telemetry. It doesn't push unsolicited software or auto-restart for updates. A mid-2010s laptop on Linux will boot in under 30 seconds and run a full office suite, browser, and media player without struggling. LibreOffice handles documents. Firefox handles the web. GIMP handles images. The hardware you already own is almost certainly capable — it just needs an OS that isn't working against it.

THE HONEST DISCLAIMER: Linux is not for everyone. If your workflow depends on specific Windows-only software, I'll tell you that upfront. But for browsing, documents, email, video, and general use — most people never notice the difference after the first week.
